Seizure first aid

Watching someone have a seizure (fit) is frightening, but most stop on their own within a couple of minutes. Simple first aid keeps the person safe until it passes.

On this page

  1. What to do
  2. What not to do
  3. Call 112 if
  4. Afterwards

What to do

  • Stay calm and stay with them. Note the time the seizure started.
  • Protect them from injury. Move hard or sharp objects away. Cushion their head with something soft, such as a folded jacket.
  • Loosen anything tight around the neck, and remove glasses.
  • Once the jerking stops, roll them onto their side (the recovery position) so saliva can drain and the airway stays open.
  • Stay until they are fully awake. Confusion and sleepiness afterwards are normal. Speak gently and reassure them.

What not to do

  • Don't put anything in their mouth. They cannot swallow their tongue, and objects can break teeth or block the airway.
  • Don't hold them down or try to stop the movements.
  • Don't give food or water until they are fully alert.
  • Don't use smelling salts, onions or shoes. They don't help.

Call 112 if

  • The seizure lasts more than five minutes
  • One seizure follows another without the person waking up in between
  • It is the person's first seizure
  • They are injured, pregnant, have diabetes, or had the seizure in water
  • They have trouble breathing, or don't wake up afterwards

Call 112 or go to your nearest emergency department. Don't wait for a clinic appointment or a reply to an email.

Afterwards

Write down what you saw: how it started, which parts of the body moved, how long it lasted and how the person was afterwards. A phone video, taken only if it is safe and doesn't delay first aid, can be very helpful to the doctor.

After a first seizure, people should not drive until a doctor has advised them. Common causes in India include neurocysticercosis, which is very treatable.
