Looking after your back

Back and neck pain are extremely common, and most episodes settle within a few weeks. The spine is strong. Staying active, rather than resting, is usually the most helpful thing you can do.

On this page

  1. What helps
  2. Common worries
  3. See a doctor if
  4. Emergency signs

What helps

  • Keep moving. Gentle walking and normal daily activity speed recovery. Bed rest for more than a day or two usually makes things worse.
  • Short-term pain relief so you can stay active. Ask a pharmacist or doctor what suits you.
  • Heat can ease muscle spasm.
  • Regular exercise such as walking, swimming, yoga or a physiotherapy programme reduces future episodes.
  • Change position often at work. No single sitting posture is perfect; moving is what matters.

Common worries

"My disc has slipped out"
Discs don't slip out of place. Bulges are common, often painless, and frequently shrink over time.
"I should avoid bending"
Bending and lifting are normal and safe for most people. Build up gradually after an episode of pain.
"My scan shows degeneration"
Wear-and-tear changes are as normal with age as grey hair, and appear on scans of many people without pain.

See a doctor if

The pain isn't improving after four to six weeks, it travels down the arm or leg with numbness or weakness, it is worse at night or at rest, or you also have fever, weight loss, or a history of cancer, TB or osteoporosis.

Emergency signs

  • Numbness around the groin, buttocks or inner thighs
  • New difficulty passing urine, or loss of bladder or bowel control
  • Weakness in both legs, or weakness that is quickly getting worse

Call 112 or go to your nearest emergency department. Don't wait for a clinic appointment or a reply to an email.
