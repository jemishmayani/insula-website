Blood thinners and brain or spine surgery

Many people take medicines that thin the blood, to prevent strokes or heart attacks. Before brain or spine surgery they often need to be paused, but stopping them without advice can be dangerous.

On this page

  1. Which medicines
  2. Why they matter
  3. Making a plan
  4. In an emergency

Which medicines

  • Antiplatelets: aspirin, clopidogrel, prasugrel, ticagrelor
  • Anticoagulants: warfarin, acenocoumarol, apixaban, rivaroxaban, dabigatran, and heparin injections
  • Some painkillers (such as ibuprofen) and supplements (such as fish oil or high-dose vitamin E) can also affect bleeding

Why they matter

Bleeding inside the skull or spinal canal, even a small amount, can press on the brain or nerves. So surgeons usually want blood clotting to be as normal as possible during and after the operation.

At the same time, these medicines protect against clots. For someone with a recent heart stent, an artificial heart valve or a recent clot, stopping them can be risky.

Making a plan

  • Never stop a blood thinner on your own. The decision is made together by your surgeon and the doctor who prescribed it.
  • Bring the exact names and doses to your appointment, or the strips themselves.
  • You'll be told when to stop, whether you need a temporary injection instead, and when to restart after surgery.

In an emergency

After any head injury or fall, tell the hospital straight away if you take a blood thinner. A scan is usually advised even if you feel well, and some medicines can be reversed quickly.
