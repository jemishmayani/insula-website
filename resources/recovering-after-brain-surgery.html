Recovering after brain surgery

Recovery after brain surgery varies a great deal depending on the operation. Your team will give you specific advice. This guide covers what is common for most people in the first weeks.

On this page

  1. Your wound
  2. How you may feel
  3. Medicines
  4. Getting back to normal
  5. Seek urgent help if

Your wound

  • Keep it clean and dry until you are told you can wash your hair, usually after a few days. Pat dry gently.
  • Don't apply oils, creams or hair dye until it has fully healed.
  • Stitches or clips are usually removed at 7 to 14 days.
  • Some swelling around the eyes and face, and numbness around the scar, are common and settle with time.

How you may feel

  • Tiredness is the most common complaint and can last weeks to months. Plan rest periods during the day.
  • Headaches usually ease week by week with simple painkillers.
  • Concentration, memory and mood can take time to return to normal. This is common and usually improves.

Medicines

Take steroids and anti-seizure medicines exactly as prescribed. Steroids are usually reduced gradually and must not be stopped suddenly. Ask before restarting any blood thinners.

Getting back to normal

  • Walk a little more each day.
  • Avoid heavy lifting, swimming and contact sport until your surgeon allows them.
  • Driving: don't drive until your doctor says it is safe. After seizures or some types of brain surgery, there are legal rules about driving.
  • Return to work gradually, often part-time at first.

Seek urgent help if

  • Increasing drowsiness, confusion, or a severe or worsening headache
  • A seizure, or new weakness, numbness or difficulty speaking
  • Fever, redness, swelling or discharge from the wound
  • Clear fluid leaking from the wound, nose or ear
  • Repeated vomiting

Call 112 or go to your nearest emergency department. Don't wait for a clinic appointment or a reply to an email.
