Recovering after spine surgery

Recovery after spine surgery is different for everyone and depends on the operation you had. Always follow your own team's advice. These are general principles that apply to many people.

On this page

  1. Moving and walking
  2. Caring for your wound
  3. Lifting, driving and work
  4. Signs to report

Moving and walking

Most people are encouraged to get up and walk soon after surgery, often the same or next day. Short, regular walks help recovery. Increase the distance gradually.

Caring for your wound

  • Keep the wound clean and dry as instructed
  • Don't apply creams unless advised
  • Ask when stitches or clips need removing

Lifting, driving and work

Your team will advise when you can lift, bend, drive and return to work. This varies widely depending on the operation and your job, so ask for specific guidance rather than relying on general rules.

Signs to report

  • Increasing redness, swelling, discharge or bleeding from the wound
  • Fever
  • New or worsening weakness or numbness
  • A headache that is worse when sitting or standing and eases lying down
  • Pain in the calf, or breathlessness

Problems with bladder or bowel control, or numbness around the groin, need emergency care.
