    Glossary

    Plain meanings for the words you may meet in scan reports, clinic letters and consent forms.

    88 terms
    ABCDEFGHIKLMNOPRSTV

    A

    Acute
    Started recently or suddenly. It describes timing, not how serious something is.
    Aneurysm
    A bulge in a weak spot of a blood vessel wall. In the brain, most never burst. Read more
    Angiogram
    A scan or X-ray test that shows blood vessels, usually after a dye is injected. Read more
    Annulus
    The tough outer ring of a spinal disc. A tear in it can let the soft centre push out. Read more
    Arachnoid
    The thin middle layer of the coverings around the brain and spinal cord.
    Atrophy
    Shrinking of a body part. Mild brain atrophy is common with age.
    Awake craniotomy
    Brain surgery where the patient is awake for part of it, so speech or movement can be checked. The scalp is numbed, and the brain itself does not feel pain. Read more

    B

    Benign
    Not cancer. A benign tumour does not spread to other parts of the body, though it can still press on nearby structures. Read more
    Biopsy
    Taking a small sample of tissue so it can be examined under a microscope.
    Brainstem
    The stalk at the base of the brain that joins it to the spinal cord. It controls breathing, heart rate and consciousness.
    Burr hole
    A small round hole drilled through the skull. Read more

    C

    Cauda equina
    The bundle of nerve roots at the bottom of the spinal canal, below the end of the spinal cord. The name means 'horse's tail'. Read more
    Cerebellum
    The part of the brain at the back, below the main brain. It controls balance and coordination.
    Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)
    The clear fluid that surrounds and cushions the brain and spinal cord. Read more
    Cervical
    To do with the neck. The cervical spine has seven vertebrae, C1 to C7.
    Chronic
    Long-lasting, usually for more than three months. It describes timing, not how serious something is.
    Clinical correlation
    A phrase in scan reports asking the doctor to compare a finding with your symptoms and examination. Read more
    Coiling
    Treating an aneurysm from inside the blood vessel by filling it with soft platinum coils. Read more
    Compression
    Pressure on a nerve or the spinal cord. Whether it matters depends on whether it fits your symptoms.
    Concussion
    A mild brain injury that causes temporary symptoms such as headache, dizziness or confusion after a blow to the head. Read more
    Contrast
    A dye injected during a scan to make blood vessels and some abnormal areas show up more clearly. Read more
    Craniotomy
    An operation in which a piece of the skull is lifted to reach the brain, then put back. Read more
    CT scan
    Computed tomography. A quick X-ray scan that shows bone and fresh bleeding well. Read more

    D

    Decompression
    Surgery that removes bone, ligament or disc to take pressure off a nerve or the spinal cord. Read more
    Degenerative changes
    Wear related to age, seen on many spine scans, including in people with no pain. Read more
    Dermatome
    The area of skin supplied by a single spinal nerve root.
    Disc
    The soft cushion between two vertebrae. It has a tough outer ring and a softer centre. Read more
    Disc bulge
    A disc that spreads a little beyond its normal edge. Very common, and often harmless. Read more
    Dura
    The tough outer covering of the brain and spinal cord.

    E

    Endoscope
    A thin tube with a camera and light, used for keyhole surgery. Read more
    Enhancement
    An area that lights up on a scan after contrast is given. It helps narrow down what something might be. Read more
    Epidural
    The space just outside the dura, the covering of the spinal cord.
    Extradural haematoma
    Blood collected between the skull and the dura, usually after a head injury. Read more

    F

    Facet joint
    One of the small paired joints at the back of the spine that link the vertebrae.
    Foramen
    An opening in bone. Spinal nerve roots leave the spine through openings called foramina. Read more
    Frontal lobe
    The front part of the brain. It helps with planning, behaviour, personality and movement.
    Fusion
    Joining two or more vertebrae so they heal into one solid bone. Read more

    G

    GCS (Glasgow Coma Scale)
    A score from 3 to 15 that measures how awake and responsive a person is, based on eyes, speech and movement. Read more
    Glioma
    A tumour that grows from the brain's own support cells. Gliomas range from slow-growing to fast-growing. Read more

    H

    Haematoma
    A collection of blood outside a blood vessel.
    Haemorrhage
    Bleeding.
    Herniation
    When part of a structure pushes out of its normal place, such as the centre of a disc. Read more
    Hydrocephalus
    A build-up of fluid inside the brain that raises pressure. Read more
    Hyperintensity
    An area that looks brighter than its surroundings on certain MRI images. Small ones are common with age. Read more

    I

    Incidental finding
    Something found by chance on a scan done for another reason. Most are harmless. Read more
    Infarct
    An area of tissue damaged because its blood supply was cut off, as in a stroke. Read more
    Intracranial pressure
    The pressure inside the skull.
    Ischaemia
    Not enough blood flow to part of the body. Read more

    K

    Kyphoplasty
    Injecting bone cement into a broken vertebra after first making a space with a small balloon. Read more

    L

    Lamina
    The flat plate of bone forming the back of each vertebra. Read more
    Laminectomy
    Removing part of the lamina to make more room for the nerves. Read more
    Lesion
    A general word for any area that looks different from normal. It does not mean cancer. Read more
    Lumbar
    To do with the lower back. The lumbar spine has five vertebrae, L1 to L5.
    Lumbar puncture
    A test in which a thin needle is placed in the lower back to measure the pressure of the spinal fluid or take a sample.

    M

    Malignant
    Cancer. A malignant tumour can grow quickly and may spread. Read more
    Meninges
    The three layers that cover the brain and spinal cord: dura, arachnoid and pia.
    Meningioma
    A usually benign tumour that grows from the meninges. Read more
    Metastasis
    A tumour that has spread from cancer elsewhere in the body. Read more
    Midline shift
    When swelling or a collection of blood pushes the brain across from its central position. It is a sign of significant pressure. Read more
    MRI scan
    Magnetic resonance imaging. A scan that uses a strong magnet and radio waves, with no X-rays, to show soft tissue in detail. Read more
    Myelopathy
    Damage to the spinal cord, often from pressure in the neck. Read more

    N

    Navigation
    A system that links the patient's scan to their position during surgery, like GPS. Read more
    Nerve root
    The part of a spinal nerve as it leaves the spinal cord and the spine.
    Neuralgia
    Sharp, often electric, pain along the path of a nerve. Read more

    O

    Occipital lobe
    The back part of the brain. It handles vision.
    Oedema
    Swelling caused by extra fluid. Around a tumour, it is often eased by steroid medicines.

    P

    Parietal lobe
    The upper back part of the brain. It handles touch, body position and spatial awareness.
    Pedicle
    The short, strong pillar of bone that joins the front and back of a vertebra. Fusion screws pass through it. Read more
    Pituitary gland
    A small gland below the brain that controls many of the body's hormones. Read more
    Prolapse
    Another word for a slipped or herniated disc. Read more

    R

    Radiculopathy
    Pain, numbness or weakness caused by pressure on a nerve root. In the leg it is often called sciatica. Read more

    S

    Sciatica
    Pain that travels from the lower back or buttock down the leg, usually from pressure on a nerve root. Read more
    Seizure
    A sudden burst of abnormal electrical activity in the brain. It can cause jerking, staring or loss of awareness. Also called a fit. Read more
    Shunt
    A thin tube that drains extra fluid from the brain to another part of the body. Read more
    Spinal cord
    The bundle of nerves running from the brainstem down the spine to about the level of the first lumbar vertebra.
    Spondylolisthesis
    When one vertebra slips forward on the one below. Read more
    Spondylosis
    Wear and tear of the spine with age.
    Stenosis
    Narrowing of a space, such as the spinal canal or a blood vessel. Read more
    Stereotactic
    Using precise three-dimensional coordinates from a scan to guide an instrument to a target. Read more
    Subarachnoid haemorrhage
    Bleeding into the space around the brain, often from a burst aneurysm. Read more
    Subdural haematoma
    Blood collected between the dura and the surface of the brain. Read more
    Syrinx
    A fluid-filled cavity inside the spinal cord. Read more

    T

    Temporal lobe
    The part of the brain at the side, near the ears. It handles hearing, memory and, usually on the left, understanding language.
    Thoracic
    To do with the chest. The thoracic spine has twelve vertebrae, T1 to T12, each joined to a rib.
    Thrombectomy
    Removing a blood clot from an artery through a thin tube, as in some strokes. Read more

    V

    Ventricles
    Fluid-filled spaces inside the brain where cerebrospinal fluid is made and flows. Read more
    Vertebra
    One of the 33 bones that make up the spine.
    Vertebroplasty
    Injecting bone cement into a broken vertebra to steady it and ease pain. Read more

    No terms match your search. Try a shorter word, or suggest a term.
