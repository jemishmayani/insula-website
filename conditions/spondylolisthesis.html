Spondylolisthesis

Spondylolisthesis means one vertebra has slipped forward over the one below it. Many people with a slip have little or no trouble, but it can cause back pain and squeeze the nerves to the legs.

What it is

There are two common types.

  • Degenerative. In older adults, usually at the L4–L5 level, worn facet joints and a weakened disc allow the vertebra to slide forward.
  • Isthmic. In younger people, usually at L5, a stress fracture in a thin part of the back of the vertebra (the pars, often from sports that bend the back repeatedly) lets the vertebra slip.

Slips are graded by how far the vertebra has moved, from grade I (less than a quarter) upward.

Treatment

Aligned

Each vertebra sits squarely on the one below, held by the disc, the small facet joints at the back and strong ligaments.

Slipping forward

Worn facet joints and a weakened disc let L4 slide forward on L5. In younger people, a stress fracture in the back of the vertebra can cause a similar slip, usually at L5.

Nerves squeezed

The slip narrows the canal like a step, squeezing the nerves. This causes back pain and leg pain, heaviness or numbness on walking, often eased by sitting or bending forward.

Decompression and fixation

If symptoms persist despite non-surgical care, the nerves are freed, and screws and rods hold the vertebrae in better alignment while a spacer in the disc space helps the bones fuse.

Symptoms

  • Lower back pain, often worse with standing and activity
  • Leg pain, heaviness, numbness or tingling on walking, eased by sitting or bending forward
  • Pain shooting down one leg if a nerve root is pinched
  • Tight hamstring muscles in younger people

How it is diagnosed

X-rays, including views taken while bending forward and backward, show the slip and whether it moves. An MRI scan shows pressure on the nerves. A CT scan shows a pars fracture clearly.

Treatment options

  • Non-surgical care helps most people: physiotherapy focused on core strength, pain relief, activity modification and sometimes injections.
  • Decompression and fusion. If symptoms seriously limit daily life despite this, an operation frees the squeezed nerves and joins the vertebrae with screws and rods, often with a spacer in the disc space so the bones fuse in a better position.

When to seek urgent help

  • Numbness around the groin or new bladder or bowel problems
  • Rapidly worsening weakness in the legs

Call 112 or go to your nearest emergency department. Don't wait for a clinic appointment or a reply to an email.

Questions to ask your doctor

  • What grade is the slip, and is it moving?
  • Are the nerves being squeezed?
  • Would decompression alone be enough, or do I need fusion?
  • Which activities are safe for me?
