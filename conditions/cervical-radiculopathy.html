Cervical radiculopathy

Cervical radiculopathy means a nerve root in the neck is being squeezed, usually by a disc bulge or a bony spur. It causes pain, tingling, numbness or weakness travelling down one arm. Each root supplies its own strip of the arm, so the pattern of symptoms shows which root is affected.

What it is

Eight pairs of nerve roots leave the spinal cord in the neck, each passing through a small opening between two vertebrae. A disc bulge (common in younger adults) or bony spurs from wear and tear (common after 50) can narrow this opening and press on the root.

This is different from cervical myelopathy, where the spinal cord itself is squeezed.

Which nerve root, which part of the arm

Nerve root

C5 root (disc between C4 and C5)

Pain and numbness over the outer shoulder and upper arm. Lifting the arm out to the side can become weak, and the biceps reflex may be reduced.

C6 root (disc between C5 and C6)

Pain runs down the outer forearm into the thumb and index finger. Bending the elbow and lifting the wrist may weaken. This is one of the two most common levels.

C7 root (disc between C6 and C7)

Pain into the middle finger, often with an ache in the back of the arm and shoulder blade. Straightening the elbow may weaken. This is the most common level.

C8 root (disc between C7 and T1)

Numbness in the ring and little fingers and along the inner forearm. Grip and fine finger movements may weaken.

Symptoms

  • Sharp, burning or aching pain spreading from the neck into the shoulder blade, arm and hand, often worse than the neck pain
  • Tingling or numbness in a particular strip of the arm and fingers
  • Weakness of particular movements, such as lifting the arm, bending or straightening the elbow, or gripping
  • Pain that eases when you rest your hand on top of your head

How it is diagnosed

An examination of feeling, strength and reflexes in the arm usually points to the root involved. An MRI scan shows the disc or bone pressing on it. Nerve conduction studies can help when the cause is unclear.

Treatment options

  • Non-surgical care. Pain relief, nerve pain medicines, physiotherapy and staying active.
  • Nerve root injection of steroid around the root can ease severe pain.
  • Surgery is considered for pain that remains severe despite treatment, or for weakness that is significant or getting worse. Options include removing the disc from the front of the neck and fusing the bones (ACDF), replacing the disc with an artificial one, or widening the opening from the back of the neck (foraminotomy).

When to seek urgent help

  • Weakness in both arms or in the legs, or difficulty walking
  • New problems with bladder or bowel control
  • Arm symptoms after a significant neck injury

Call 112 or go to your nearest emergency department. Don't wait for a clinic appointment or a reply to an email.
