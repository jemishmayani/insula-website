Neurocysticercosis

Neurocysticercosis is an infection of the brain with the larval stage of the pork tapeworm. It is one of the most common causes of seizures (fits) in India, and in most people it is very treatable.

What it is

A person becomes infected by swallowing tapeworm eggs, which come from the stool of someone carrying the adult tapeworm. Eggs spread through contaminated hands, water or uncooked food such as poorly washed vegetables. Vegetarians can be infected too.

The eggs hatch in the gut, and the larvae travel through the blood and settle in tissues, including the brain, where they form small cysts. Each cyst passes through stages over months to years before it dies and leaves a small calcified spot.

The life of a cyst in the brain

Stage

A living cyst

The larva of the pork tapeworm sits in a thin-walled bubble of clear fluid, its head (scolex) visible as a dot. It hides from the immune system, so it often causes no symptoms at this stage.

Dying and inflamed

As the larva dies, its fluid turns cloudy and the brain reacts. Swelling forms around the cyst and irritates the nearby brain, which is when seizures most often begin.

Shrinking

The cyst collapses into a small, firm nodule and the swelling settles. Many people stop having seizures at this point.

Calcified

What remains is a tiny chalky spot. Most cause no trouble, but in some people it can keep triggering seizures, so anti-seizure medicine may be continued.

Symptoms

  • Seizures are the most common symptom, often starting in one part of the body or causing brief jerking
  • Headaches
  • Rarely, when cysts block the flow of brain fluid, severe headache, vomiting and drowsiness from raised pressure (hydrocephalus)

Many people with cysts have no symptoms at all.

How it is diagnosed

A CT or MRI scan shows the cysts and their stage. A living cyst can show a tiny dot inside, the head of the larva, which is very characteristic. Calcified spots are best seen on CT. Blood tests can support the diagnosis, and an eye examination is done before treatment to check for cysts in the eye.

Treatment options

  • Anti-seizure medicines control fits, usually for at least a year or two after the cysts have resolved.
  • Antiparasitic medicines such as albendazole, given with steroids to calm the inflammation as the cysts die, help living cysts resolve faster in many people.
  • Surgery is needed only occasionally, for example to remove a cyst blocking the fluid pathways, or to place a shunt for hydrocephalus.

Preventing it

Wash hands with soap after using the toilet and before eating, drink safe water, wash vegetables and fruit well, and cook pork thoroughly. Family members may be checked for the adult tapeworm, which is easily treated.

When to seek urgent help

  • A seizure lasting more than five minutes, or repeated seizures without waking up in between
  • Severe headache with vomiting, drowsiness or blurred vision
  • A first seizure in an adult

Call 112 or go to your nearest emergency department. Don't wait for a clinic appointment or a reply to an email.

Questions to ask your doctor

  • What stage are the cysts at?
  • Do I need antiparasitic medicine, or only anti-seizure medicine?
  • How long will I need to take medicines?
  • When is it safe to drive again?
