Cauda equina syndrome

Cauda equina syndrome is a spinal emergency. It needs to be assessed in hospital the same day, because delay can cause permanent problems with bladder, bowel and sexual function.

What it is

At the bottom of the spine, the nerves spread out into a bundle that looks a little like a horse's tail, which is what cauda equina means in Latin. These nerves control the bladder, bowel, sexual function and parts of the legs.

If something presses hard on this bundle, most often a large slipped disc, the nerves can be damaged quickly.

Room for the nerves

Below the end of the spinal cord, nerve roots hang in the canal like a horse's tail.

A large disc prolapse

A big prolapse pushes back into the canal and starts crowding the nerve roots.

Cauda equina syndrome

The roots controlling the bladder, bowel and saddle area are squeezed. Numbness in the saddle area or new bladder problems mean hospital today.

Warning signs

  • Numbness or altered feeling around the groin, genitals, buttocks or inner thighs (the area that would touch a saddle)
  • New difficulty passing urine, or not being able to feel when the bladder is full
  • Loss of control of the bladder or bowel
  • New problems with sexual function
  • Weakness or numbness in both legs, often with back pain and sciatica

Any one of the first three, especially alongside back or leg pain, needs emergency assessment.

What to do

Go to an emergency department straight away and say that you are worried about cauda equina syndrome. An urgent MRI scan is needed. If it is confirmed, surgery to relieve the pressure on the nerves is usually done as soon as possible.

After treatment

Recovery depends largely on how much nerve damage happened before the pressure was relieved. Some people recover fully, while others have lasting bladder, bowel or sexual problems that need ongoing support. This is why acting quickly matters so much.

When to seek urgent help

  • Numbness around the groin, buttocks or inner thighs
  • Difficulty passing urine or not feeling when your bladder is full
  • Loss of bladder or bowel control
  • Weakness in both legs

Call 112 or go to your nearest emergency department. Don't wait for a clinic appointment or a reply to an email.

Questions to ask your doctor

  • Has the pressure on the nerves been fully relieved?
  • What should I watch for during recovery?
  • What support is available for bladder or bowel problems?
