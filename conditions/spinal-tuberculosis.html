Spinal tuberculosis

Spinal tuberculosis is a TB infection in the bones of the spine. It is common in India, it develops slowly, and with the right treatment most people recover well.

What it is

Tuberculosis usually affects the lungs, but it can spread through the blood to other parts of the body, including the spine. This is sometimes called Pott's spine. The infection can weaken the bones and, if untreated, cause the spine to bend forward or press on the nerves.

How untreated spinal TB damages the spine

EarlyMonthsUntreated

Early infection

TB usually starts at the front of two neighbouring vertebrae and spreads to the disc between them. Back pain, fever and weight loss come on slowly.

Bone softening

The infection eats into the front of the vertebrae and destroys the disc. A collection of pus, an abscess, can form beside the spine.

Collapse and hump

The weakened fronts of the vertebrae crumble while the back of the spine holds, so the spine bends sharply forward into a hump. The spinal cord can be stretched and compressed over the angle. Starting anti-TB medicines early prevents this.

Symptoms

  • Back or neck pain that builds up over weeks or months, often worse at night
  • Fever, night sweats, weight loss and tiredness
  • Later, a visible hump or bend in the back
  • Weakness, numbness or tingling in the legs if the nerves are affected

How it is diagnosed

An MRI scan shows the infection and any pressure on the nerves. Blood tests help, and a biopsy, taking a small sample from the spine, is often needed to confirm TB and check which medicines will work.

Treatment options

  • Anti-TB medicines are the main treatment, usually taken for many months. Completing the full course is essential, even when you feel better, to prevent the infection returning or becoming drug-resistant.
  • A brace may be used to support the spine while it heals.
  • Surgery may be needed if there is pressure on the spinal cord or nerves, a worsening deformity, an unstable spine, or if the diagnosis is uncertain.

In India, TB testing and treatment are available free of charge through government health services.

When to seek urgent help

  • New weakness or numbness in the legs
  • Difficulty walking, or new bladder or bowel problems

Call 112 or go to your nearest emergency department. Don't wait for a clinic appointment or a reply to an email.

Questions to ask your doctor

  • How long will I need to take the medicines?
  • Do I need surgery, or will medicines be enough?
  • How will we check that the treatment is working?
  • Should my family be tested for TB?
