Osteoporotic spine fracture

With osteoporosis, bones become thin and fragile. A vertebra can crack and collapse after a minor fall, lifting something, or sometimes with no clear injury at all. It is a common cause of sudden back pain in older people, and a warning sign that the bones need treatment.

What it is

Inside each vertebra is a honeycomb of bone that carries the weight of the body. In osteoporosis this honeycomb thins. The front of the vertebra, which carries the most load, can collapse into a wedge shape. This is called a vertebral compression fracture.

It is most common in women after the menopause, older men, and people who have taken steroid tablets for a long time.

Treatment

Thin, porous bone

With osteoporosis, the honeycomb of bone inside each vertebra thins out. The bone looks normal from outside but has lost much of its strength.

A crack at the front

The front of the vertebra carries most of the load. A fall, lifting or even a cough can crack it, causing sudden back pain.

Wedge collapse

The front crushes into a wedge while the back stays tall, so the spine tips forward. Several fractures can cause loss of height and a stooped back.

Kyphoplasty

Most fractures heal in 6 to 12 weeks with pain relief, a brace and gradual activity. If pain stays severe, a small balloon can be passed through a needle into the vertebra and inflated, lifting the crushed front back towards its normal height. The balloon is removed and bone cement fills the space and sets within minutes. Treating the osteoporosis itself helps prevent the next fracture.

Symptoms

  • Sudden back pain, usually in the middle or lower back, worse when standing or walking and eased by lying down
  • Loss of height
  • A gradually stooped or rounded back after several fractures

Some fractures cause little pain and are found by chance on an X-ray.

How it is diagnosed

An X-ray shows the collapsed vertebra. An MRI scan shows whether a fracture is new, and helps rule out other causes such as infection or cancer. A bone density (DEXA) scan confirms osteoporosis.

Treatment options

  • Most fractures heal over 6 to 12 weeks with pain relief, a short period of reduced activity, sometimes a brace, and then a gradual return to walking.
  • Vertebroplasty or kyphoplasty. If pain stays severe, bone cement can be injected into the fractured vertebra through a needle in the back to stabilise it.
  • Surgery with screws and rods is rarely needed, for example when a fracture presses on the nerves or the spine becomes unstable.
  • Treating the osteoporosis with calcium, vitamin D, exercise and bone-strengthening medicines reduces the risk of the next fracture, which is high after the first.

When to seek urgent help

  • New weakness, numbness or tingling in the legs
  • New problems with bladder or bowel control
  • Back pain with fever, weight loss, or a history of cancer

Call 112 or go to your nearest emergency department. Don't wait for a clinic appointment or a reply to an email.
