    History

    From holes in the skull to GPS in theatre

    A short history of brain and spine surgery: the people, the tools and the scans that made today's operations possible.

    Ancient times

    1. Thousands of years ago

      Holes in the skull

      Skulls from many ancient cultures have neatly cut holes, a practice called trephination. Many show healed bone around the edge, which means people survived the operation. Why it was done is not certain. It may have been for head injuries, headaches or spiritual reasons.

    2. About 1600 BCE

      The first written head injury cases

      The Edwin Smith Papyrus from ancient Egypt describes injuries to the head and spine in a calm, practical way. It is one of the earliest medical texts to mention the brain.

    3. About 1000 CE

      Instruments drawn and described

      In Córdoba, the surgeon Al-Zahrawi wrote a large medical work that described and drew hundreds of surgical instruments, including tools for the skull.

    Learning the map

    1. 1664

      The arteries of the brain

      Thomas Willis, an English doctor, published a detailed book on the brain. The ring of arteries at its base is still called the circle of Willis.

    2. 1861

      Speech has a home

      Paul Broca, a French doctor, showed that damage to a small area of the left frontal lobe takes away speech. It was strong early evidence that different parts of the brain do different jobs.

    3. 1879 to 1884

      Finding a tumour from symptoms

      William Macewen in Glasgow and, a few years later, Rickman Godlee in London removed brain tumours that had been located only from the patient's symptoms and examination. No scans existed yet.

    Seeing inside

    1. 1895

      X-rays

      Wilhelm Röntgen discovered X-rays in Germany on 8 November 1895. Within weeks, he had an image of the bones of his wife's hand. X-rays showed the skull, but not the brain.

      The first X-ray, on Wikimedia Commons

    2. 1900s to 1930s

      Harvey Cushing makes surgery safer

      Harvey Cushing in the United States changed brain surgery from a very dangerous operation into a safer one. He recorded blood pressure during operations, used small silver clips to stop bleeding, and with William Bovie brought electrical cautery into the operating room.

    3. 1918

      Air outlines the brain

      Walter Dandy replaced the fluid inside the brain with air. On X-ray, the air showed the shape of the fluid spaces. If they were pushed aside, something was pressing on them.

    4. 1927

      The blood vessels appear

      Egas Moniz in Portugal injected dye into the neck arteries and took X-rays. For the first time, doctors could see the brain's blood vessels.

      Egas Moniz, on Wikimedia Commons

    5. 1937

      The first aneurysm clip

      Walter Dandy closed the neck of a brain aneurysm with a small silver clip. Clipping is still used today, with far better clips and microscopes.

    Precision

    1. 1947

      Coordinates for the brain

      Ernest Spiegel and Henry Wycis described a frame that used three-dimensional coordinates to guide a needle to a deep target in the human brain. This is called stereotactic surgery.

    2. 1950s

      Shunts for hydrocephalus

      Reliable one-way valves made it possible to drain extra brain fluid into the body through a tube. Many children with hydrocephalus who would not have survived could now grow up.

    3. 1957 to 1960s

      The operating microscope

      Surgeons began operating with a microscope. Gazi Yaşargil in Zurich developed microneurosurgery in the 1960s, with fine instruments that made deep brain surgery much safer.

    4. 1971

      The first CT scan

      Godfrey Hounsfield's scanner made its first image of a patient's brain in London on 1 October 1971. It showed a tumour directly. Bleeding and strokes could now be seen in minutes.

      Godfrey Hounsfield, on Wikimedia Commons

    5. 1977 to 1980s

      MRI

      The first MRI scans of the human body were made in the late 1970s, and MRI entered hospitals in the 1980s. It shows the brain, spinal cord and nerves in fine detail, with no X-rays.

      An MRI of the brain, on Wikimedia Commons

    Today

    1. 1986

      Navigation

      Frameless navigation linked the patient's scan to the surgeon's instruments, so the surgeon could see their exact position on the scan during the operation, like GPS.

    2. 1987

      Deep brain stimulation

      Alim-Louis Benabid in Grenoble showed that gentle electrical stimulation deep in the brain could stop a tremor. DBS is now used for Parkinson's disease and other movement disorders.

    3. 1991

      Coils for aneurysms

      Guido Guglielmi's detachable coils allowed aneurysms to be treated from inside the blood vessel, through a thin tube passed from the groin, with no opening in the skull.

    4. 1990s to today

      Mapping the wiring

      Functional MRI shows the areas used for speech and movement, and tractography shows the pathways that connect them. Endoscopes reach the pituitary through the nose. Some theatres can scan during the operation to check what has been removed.

      Tractography of brain pathways, on Wikimedia Commons
